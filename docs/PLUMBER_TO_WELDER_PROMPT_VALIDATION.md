### Five complete fictional job-description fixtures

Each has a different fictional employer ID; all locations are Toronto/GTA for this
exercise. Counts describe this constructed sample only.

**J1 — Welding Trainee / Fabrication Assistant, Test Employer A**

Day-shift trainee position helping prepare materials, measure components and
maintain an orderly work area. Welding is learned under an assigned qualified
trainer; no independent welding is expected at entry. Applicants must have
secondary school completion and experience measuring materials and using hand
tools. Previous welding experience is not required. Paid safety orientation and
supervised welding instruction are supplied before welding tasks. Interested
applicants must confirm they accept this training arrangement. No welding ticket
is required at application. This is a trainee/support role, not a qualified-welder vacancy.

**J2 — Junior MIG Welder, Test Employer B**

Day-shift work producing simple mild-steel assemblies from fabrication drawings.
Applicants must have either completed an introductory MIG course or have six
months of relevant MIG work experience. A supervised practical assessment must
be passed before appointment. Measurement skills and basic welding-symbol reading
are required. TIG experience is preferred, not required. The employer provides
task-specific safety induction, but this is not a no-experience training position.

**J3 — Structural Welder, Test Employer C**

Fabricate structural assemblies to drawings and the employer's welding procedures.
Two years of relevant structural-welding experience and demonstrated flux-cored
welding are required. Applicants must read welding symbols and meet the
project-specific welder qualification before assignment; its exact scope will be
supplied by the employer. MIG experience is helpful. The job requires independent
work after induction. A general workshop course alone does not satisfy this role's
experience condition. Afternoon shifts are required.

**J4 — TIG Welder, Stainless Fabrication, Test Employer D**

Day-shift role making stainless assemblies, checking dimensions and weld finish,
and following fabrication drawings. At least one year of professional stainless
TIG welding is required. Applicants undergo an employer practical assessment.
MIG is optional. The employer values measurement and fitting skills but requires
the stated TIG capability separately. Company process and safety induction are
provided; they are not a substitute for the requested prior experience.

**J5 — Experienced Pipe Welder, Test Employer E**

Perform specified pressure-piping welding work, interpret welding procedures and
complete required inspection records. Three years of relevant pipe-welding
experience are required. Applicants must demonstrate the processes and positions
needed by the assignment and hold its applicable authorization before regulated
work. The advert does not identify the exact authorization or procedure scope;
these must be confirmed with the employer and appropriate authority. Plumbing
installation or soldering experience alone does not satisfy the welding-experience
condition. Shift arrangements are not specified.

### What changes the decision

- J1 can be pursued as a trainee application on these facts; training and employer
  selection remain ahead, not guaranteed employment or independent work readiness.
- J2 requires evidence of one entry alternative and its practical assessment;
  plumbing tenure cannot be relabeled as MIG experience.
- J3-J5 have specialist experience/qualification conditions not supported by this
  profile. J3 also conflicts with the day-shift preference. These conditions do
  not define all welding jobs.
- Ask once whether practical welding training or professional work was omitted,
  specifying process, material, supervision and assessment context.

### Next steps

1. **Confirm the starting point.** Identify any existing welding training or assessed

2. **Pursue a suitable supervised entry route.** For this fixture, discuss J1's

3. **If MIG remains unestablished and J2-type work is chosen, undertake supervised
   process-specific training.** Do not prescribe unsupervised home welding.
   Done when a qualified instructor records the practical skills demonstrated.
4. **Check role-specific readiness.** Meet any required practical assessment and

   replace professional experience requirements.
5. **Reassess against the chosen entry-level roles.** Present plumbing strengths and

## Findings about the prompt

**Qualified result: the core assessment approach can express this case usefully;
it is not yet validated as reliable production behavior.**

The exercise preserved transferable strengths, distinguished trainee access from
welder readiness, respected alternatives and specialist boundaries, and generated
a practical plan without IT vocabulary. The checks above show the intended answer
changes rather than assuming every transition is either easy or aspirational.

Two omissions in the prompt deserve refinement before implementation:

1. **Safe supervised practice and professional sign-off.** The existing prompt
   distinguishes projects from employment but is framed heavily around software.
   For hazardous or safety-critical work, a practice project is not automatically
   an appropriate recommendation, and self-assessment cannot authorize work.
2. **Jurisdiction and credential scope.** The input includes location, but the
   prompt does not explicitly require separating legal authorization, employer
   qualification tests and optional credentials. It also lacks an explicit rule
   against carrying requirements from one jurisdiction into another.

Proposed concise addition, NOT applied to the prompt during this test:

> Where work is hazardous or regulated, recommend appropriately supervised training
> and assessment; never treat a self-directed project as authorization to practice.
> Separate verified jurisdiction/task-specific authorization, employer requirements
> and optional credentials. If authoritative information is absent, flag the exact
> check needed rather than invent a requirement or assume eligibility.

These are generic safeguards, not a plumber/welder-specific ruleset. A trade expert
should review this reference case. Then run the frozen prompt through the actual
configured model across repeated and held-out cases. The current conversation
cannot establish that another model will reproduce these distinctions.
